    01 — Who we are

    A college team building a rover that thinks for itself.

    Team AANYA is a group of students from RV College of Engineering (RVCE), Bengaluru who wanted to build a real field robot, not a kit. Six wheels, one arm, one brain, and a lot of late nights. We designed the chassis in CAD, built it by hand, wired every motor driver and wrote the control stack straight against the Linux kernel.

    RV College of EngineeringDepartment of Mechanical Engineering · Bengaluru

    The result is AANYA: a rugged, universal mobile platform that drives on six wheels, carries a multi-axis manipulator, and runs its intelligence on an onboard NVIDIA Jetson. Our goal is a rover that can see, map, and navigate on its own, while a human can always take back control in an instant.

    02 — The machine

    Meet AANYA.

    Tap the markers to explore the subsystems.

    SUBSYSTEM

    Select a marker

    Every part of AANYA was designed and integrated by the team. Pick a marker on the rover to see how it works.

      ◎

      Drivetrain

      6WD on a rocker-bogie suspension with a differential, so all six wheels stay planted. Skid-steer: left and right banks drive differentially for zero-radius turns.

      ⚡

      Power stage

      3× Cytron MDD10A dual-channel NMOS H-bridges drive all six brushed DC motors from a dedicated high-current battery rail.

      ✦

      Manipulator

      Multi-axis robotic arm driven by a PCA9685 12-bit PWM servo controller over an isolated I2C bus.

      ▣

      Edge brain

      NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano on Ubuntu, driving motors directly through Linux kernel sysfs PWM with no microcontroller in between.

      ◉

      Perception

      Camera stream, 360° LiDAR point cloud and GPS telemetry fused into one live picture of the world. In progress.

      ⇄

      Control modes

      AI Pilot for autonomous navigation, and Manual Overdrive: a gamepad that instantly overrides the AI for safety. In progress.

      03 — Engineering log

      Problems we hit.
      How we beat them.

      BUG 001 · POWER

      The rover kept killing its own brain.

      Problem
      Hard skid-steer turns pulled huge transient currents. The logic rail sagged and the Jetson crashed mid-manoeuvre.
      Root cause
      Motors and compute shared one supply, so every current spike from the drivetrain hit the processor too.
      Fix
      Dual-rail power segregation. A high-current pack feeds the motor drivers directly; an independent clean 5 V / 3 A line powers the Jetson. Both share a single ground reference at header Pin 6 to avoid ground loops.
      BUG 002 · KERNEL

      “Device or resource busy.”

      Problem
      Driving motors through Python Jetson.GPIO kept failing with a busy-device error on header Pins 32 and 33.
      Root cause
      The device tree had those pins locked to special-function (audio) duties, so the OS refused to hand them over.
      Fix
      Reconfigured the 40-pin header with NVIDIA's jetson-io.py to free Pins 32/33 as hardware PWM, then dropped the high-level library and wrote straight to the kernel's sysfs PWM interface for deterministic, low-latency control. No Arduino, no ESP32.
      aanya@jetson: ~
